If you have never touched a surfboard, the word "coaching" can sound like a lot. It is not. Good **surf coaching for complete beginners in Bouznika** is mostly calm repetition: a few simple skills, taught in the right order, in water that gives you room to fail safely. This guide explains how we run it, what a coach is looking at while you flap around, and how to decide what suits you.

## What "coaching" means when you have never surfed

A lot of first-timers imagine a coach as someone who pushes you onto a wave and shouts. Real coaching is quieter than that. A coach is making dozens of small decisions for you that you cannot yet make yourself:

- Which part of the beach is safe today
- Whether the waves are too big for your level
- When to paddle, when to wait, and when to get out
- Which correction to give first, because you cannot fix five things at once

That last point is the heart of it. Complete beginners usually have a list of mistakes: feet too far back, head down, arms too tired. A coach picks the one that matters most for the next wave. That is why people often improve faster with a local coach than by copying videos.

### Why the beach matters

Bouznika is a sandy Atlantic beach break between Rabat and Casablanca. Falling onto sand-bottom water is a very different experience from falling near rock. For a nervous beginner that matters more than any gear. We explain the break in more detail in our [beginner guide to learning to surf in Bouznika](/blog/learn-to-surf-bouznika-beginners).

## How we do it in Bouznika

Our beginner lessons are taught with Association Bouznika SurfSchool, and the structure is the same whether you are a solo traveller or a family. A typical 1.5 hour session runs like this.

### 1. Meet, talk, and check the water

We start on the sand. We ask about your swimming, any injuries, and how you feel about the ocean. Then we look at the sea together. Some days the whitewater is gentle and perfect. Other days we shorten the water time or change the plan. We would rather tell you honestly than sell a session that does not suit the conditions.

### 2. The land lesson

Before the water, you practise on the sand:

- How to lie on the board so it glides rather than sinks
- How to paddle with long, relaxed strokes
- The pop-up, done slowly and then faster
- Where to look (forward, not at your feet)
- How to fall safely and protect your head

This takes less time than people expect, but it is the part that saves your session. If you skip it, the water becomes chaos.

### 3. Whitewater first

In the water we stay near the shore, pushing you into broken waves so you feel the board accelerate. The goal here is not to stand up perfectly. The goal is to feel timing: when the wave picks you up, when to paddle, when to pop.

### 4. Small adjustments, one at a time

As you improve, the coach gives one correction after each wave. Maybe it is "chest up on the paddle." Maybe it is "back foot over the tail." You hear one thing, try it, and the next wave tells us whether it worked.

### 5. A calm finish

We finish while you still have energy. Tired beginners make more mistakes and enjoy the ocean less. Ending early on a good wave is better than squeezing out another poor one.

## What a coach is watching that you cannot see

Most of what helps you happens before you notice it. During your first sessions a coach is tracking:

- **Your breathing and tension.** If your shoulders are up near your ears, you will tire fast. A cue to breathe out slowly often fixes it.
- **Where you sit on the board.** Too far forward and the nose digs. Too far back and you will not catch the wave.
- **Your paddle rhythm.** Short, frantic strokes waste energy. Longer strokes make you faster.
- **Your fear signals.** Looking at the shore, gripping the rails, or stopping mid-paddle usually tell us you are anxious. We slow down, reset, and go again.
- **Other water users.** The coach manages space so you can focus on yourself.

None of this needs you to understand the theory. It just means you are not alone with your own mistakes.

## Private or group: which suits a complete beginner?

This is the most common question we get, and there is no single right answer.

**Private lessons** are 300 dh for 1.5 hours. The coach is with you the whole time, repeating cues and picking each wave. This tends to suit:

- Nervous swimmers or people who dislike crowds
- Parents learning alongside a child
- Anyone who wants faster feedback with fewer people in the water

**Group lessons** are 200 dh for 1.5 hours. You share the coach with other learners, which means more waiting, but also more energy and shared laughs. This tends to suit:

- Confident, social beginners
- Friends travelling together
- Budget-conscious learners who want a first taste

## What to expect in your first three sessions

Everyone progresses differently, so treat this as a rough shape rather than a promise.

### Session one: learn the language

You learn the board, the paddle, the pop-up, and the feeling of whitewater. Many people stand at least a few times. Others spend the session getting comfortable lying on the board. Both are fine.

### Session two: timing

The pop-up gets smoother. You start choosing which wave to take, and you begin to see why position matters. This is often the session where people stop thinking of the board as an enemy.

### Session three: first real control

You start steering slightly, staying standing longer, and recovering from small mistakes. Some people are ready to move beyond whitewater. Others benefit from another round, and that is normal.

## Common beginner mistakes a coach will catch

These are the ones we correct most often:

1. **Looking down at the board.** Your body follows your eyes. Look at the beach.
2. **Popping up with knees first.** It feels natural but throws you off balance. Hands flat, feet through, one smooth move.
3. **Paddling too late.** The wave passes before you start. A coach watches the wave for you and calls the moment.
4. **Standing too tall.** Bent knees keep you stable.
5. **Going out before the basics click.** The urge to chase bigger waves is strong. We hold back until you can control the small ones.

## Choosing a coach: practical questions to ask

Wherever you learn, a good school should be able to answer these without hesitation:

- How many people will be in the water with one coach?
- What is included in the price (board, wetsuit, rash vest)?
- What happens if conditions are poor on the day?
- Will the coach explain things on the sand before the water?
- Do they take safety and swimming ability seriously?

## How to prepare so your coaching time counts

A few small habits make your first lesson much better:

- **Eat lightly** an hour or two before, not a heavy meal.
- **Drink water**, even if the day is cool.
- **Sunscreen** matters on the Atlantic coast, even when it is cloudy.
- **Wear something you can swim in**, and bring a towel and dry clothes.
- **Tell your coach everything** relevant: past injuries, fear of deep water, glasses or contact lenses.
- **Arrive a bit early** so the land lesson does not feel rushed.

You do not need to do any "pre-training." A little general mobility in shoulders and hips helps, but showing up rested and relaxed helps more.
