People overpack for a **first surf lesson Morocco what to bring** search as if they are crossing the Sahara. You are going to a beach in Bouznika for ninety minutes. The ocean does not care about your extra rash vest in three colours.

Here is what actually helps, what we already have at [surf coaching](/surf-coach), and what you should leave behind so you are not babysitting a pile of stuff while you try to stand up.

## We provide the two things beginners think they must buy

**Board.** Soft-top, sized to you. You do not need to show up with a 6'2" shortboard your cousin used in France.

**Wetsuit.** Fitted from the school. If you already own one that is not a souvenir hanging off your shoulders, bring it. We take 50 dh off the lesson. If it is too big, you will flush cold water on every wave — wear ours instead.

That is the expensive kit. Everything else is comfort and skin.

## What to wear to the beach

- **Swimsuit you can wear under a wetsuit** (sports bikini, trunks, or a one-piece). Cotton underwear under rubber is a miserable idea.
- **Flip-flops or sandals** for the walk on hot sand and the car park.
- **A towel** that can get sandy. You will want it after.
- **A simple long-sleeve shirt** for after the session if the wind comes up.

Do not wear jewellery you care about. Rings and waves are a bad mix. A cheap leash-safe watch is fine; a dress watch is not.

## Sun, salt, and skin

The Moroccan Atlantic sun is not gentle just because there are clouds. You will be face-down paddling.

- **Reef-safe or at least water-resistant sunscreen**, applied 20 minutes before we start, including the part in your hairline and the backs of your knees.
- **Zinc** on the nose and cheeks if you burn easily.
- **Leave-in conditioner or a wide-tooth comb** for after — optional, but salt in long hair is no joke.

If you have a cut, cover it. Salt finds every nick. If you ding yourself on the board (rare on foam, still possible), tell us.

## Water, food, and energy

Bring **a bottle of water**. Surfing feels cold and wet, so people forget they are sweating into the suit.

Eat light. Fruit, yogurt, bread, a small omelette — not a feast. If you are prone to cramps, a banana is a cliché because it works often enough.

Coffee is fine. Three coffees and no food is how your hands shake on the first pop-up.

## What not to bring

- **A hard shortboard “to progress faster.”** You will not. You will get in the way of your own learning.
- **Fins you want to screw in yourself.** School boards are set up.
- **A waterproof speaker.** Let the ocean be loud.
- **Your whole camera bag.** One phone in a cheap pouch is enough if you need a photo after. During the lesson, your hands are for paddling.
- **Passports, drones, laptops.** The beach is not a locker.

If you are staying at our [oceanfront villa](/surf-guiding), leave valuables in the house. If you drove from Casablanca for the morning, lock the car and take the key.

## Body and health — the quiet packing list

Tell us **before** we paddle if you cannot swim well, if your shoulders are injured, if you get panic in the water, or if you had surgery recently. There is no shame in a slower start. There is a problem if I find out when you are already outside.

If you get seasick in whitewater (it happens), look at the horizon between waves and breathe. We can keep you in smaller soup.

Contact lenses can work; plenty of people surf in them. A cheap pair of sport glasses with a strap is an alternative. Seeing the wave matters.

## A simple bag checklist

1. Swimsuit  
2. Towel  
3. Water  
4. Sunscreen  
5. Hair tie if you need one  
6. Change of dry clothes for the drive home  
7. Optional: your own wetsuit, 50 dh off  

That is the list. Everything else is either provided or in the way.
