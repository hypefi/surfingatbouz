**Pressure ding vs crack when to repair your surfboard** is the question every traveller asks while standing in a car park, board in one hand, phone flashlight in the other.

Both can look like “just a mark.” Only one is obviously drinking the ocean. The other is a quiet structural insult. In Bouznika I see both every week. Here is how we sort them in the shop — prices and booking are on [board repair](/board-repair).

## The crack (open glass)

This is the honest hole.

- You see **foam** (white, yellow, or brown)
- The glass is **chipped or folded**
- A fingernail **catches**
- After a session the ding feels **wet** or the board feels **heavier**
- You hear a **pssst** of air when you press around it (not always, but when you do, stop guessing)

Open glass on a PU board is a race. Foam is a sponge. Salt water plus sun plus two more sessions is how a 200 dh ding becomes a delam quote.

**First aid:** rinse with fresh water if you have it, dry, tape, come in. Do not “one more hour.”

Small open dings: 100 dh. Moderate: 200 dh. Rails and tail blocks are often the moderate column even when they look tiny from the beach.

## The pressure ding (crushed foam, skin still there)

The deck has a **thumbprint**. The glass might still be glossy. No obvious hole.

What happened: impact compressed the foam. The sandwich is now thinner there. On a wave, that spot flexes differently. On a thin performance board, it can flatten the rocker in a way you feel even if you cannot explain it.

On a thick soft-top, a knee dent is ugly and sometimes still fine for a beginner week. On a 28-litre shortboard, a deep chest-to-deck pressure ding is a repair — 150 dh on our menu — before it star-cracks and becomes an open job anyway.

## The in-between: hairline and spidering

White stars around a dent mean the glass already failed even if foam is not showing yet. Treat that as a **crack in progress**. Tape is theatre.

If the board is epoxy, hairlines can be sneakier — the cloth behaves differently. Do not diagnose from a meme. Bring it. [PU vs epoxy](/blog/pu-vs-epoxy-ding-repair-morocco) is the chemistry companion to this article.

## A two-minute beach test

1. Dry the board. Wet gloss hides everything.  
2. Look in **side light**, not noon glare.  
3. Press around the mark with a thumb. Soft, wet, or crunchy is bad.  
4. Check the **inside of the rail** — people stare at the logo and miss the hit.  
5. Smell. A sour, old-wet-foam smell is information.

If you are still unsure, that is a 150–200 dh question, not a philosophical one.

## When we tell you to keep surfing

Cosmetic scuffs on a sacrificial foam school board: surf. A sealed, shallow pressure mark on a log you barely stand on: maybe surf, watch it.

When we tell you to stop: anything open, anything on a rail that is folding, anything that got heavier overnight, any fin box that moved (that is 500 dh and not a ding debate).

## Travellers and the last-day problem

You ding it on Thursday, you fly Saturday. Tell me Thursday. A small dry crack can sometimes be closed. A soaked rail cannot be honestly cured in a night just because the airline is unmovable.

If you live in Casa and surf Bouznika on weekends, do not collect five “I’ll deal with it” dents. Ten-plus dings on one board is 40% off, but that discount is a monument to neglect.

Pressure ding or crack — both are the ocean leaving a note. Read it in daylight, not after the foam has had a drink. Then walk it to the shop.
