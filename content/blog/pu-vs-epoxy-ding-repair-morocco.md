**PU vs epoxy ding repair Morocco** sounds like shaper-forum talk. It becomes practical the moment you open a hole in Bouznika and someone in the car park says “just use the same sun-cure you used on your brother’s board.”

Those two constructions are different sandwiches. I repair both here. The [ding menu](/board-repair) is the same shop; the chemistry inside the hole is not.

## PU (polyurethane foam, polyester resin) — the classic

This is the traditional surfboard most people still picture: foam blank, wooden stringer, polyester resin, fibreglass cloth.

**How it fails on this coast**

- Dings **open easily** on rails and noses
- Foam **yellows** in UV and shows every old repair
- Water **wicks fast** once glass is broken
- A neglected board gets **heavy and soft** — the beginning of delam

**How we repair it**

Dry the foam. Really dry it. Cut away rotten stuff. Fill, cloth, resin, hot coat, sand, maybe gloss. If you rush the dry, you have trapped a swamp. That swamp comes back as a brown ghost two weeks later.

PU is honest. It shows damage. That is annoying and also useful.

## Epoxy (often EPS foam, epoxy resin) — the lighter cousin

Many “epoxy boards” are EPS foam with epoxy resin and a different glass schedule. They can take a knock that would have opened a PU rail.

**How it fails anyway**

- **Pressure dings** and core crush (see [pressure ding vs crack](/blog/pressure-ding-vs-crack-surfboard-repair))
- **Delamination** of the sandwich — a hollow tap
- Cracks that look small and run **further than you think**
- Fins and boxes that were never happy in a light core

**How we repair it**

Wrong solvents and polyester “because that is what I have” can melt or poorly bond EPS. This is the amateur-kit disaster. In the shop we use materials that match the core. Cure times and sanding feel different. You cannot will it to behave like a 1998 PU.

## Soft-tops and school boards

The boards in [lessons](/surf-coach) are foamies for a reason. They still ding, peel, and drink water at leash plugs. Repair is possible; replacement is sometimes more honest if the skin is a banana peel. We will say which.

## Shaping and restoration, briefly

People bring yellowed PUs and ask for a full restore — new glass, paint, the board their uncle shaped in the 90s. That is **shaping-adjacent work**: fairing, cloth, colour, patience. It is not a 100 dh ding. It is also some of the most satisfying work in the shop, because Morocco is full of boards that still have a good rocker under a bad decade of sun.

If you want paint or a restore, say so up front. If you want to surf this weekend, we close the hole first.

## What you should tell the repairer

- Brand and roughly **what year** if you know  
- **PU or epoxy** if you know — or “I don’t know”  
- Did it **sit wet in a bag**  
- Do you need it **before a flight**

Photos in shade, rail close-ups, and one full-board shot. Then [WhatsApp](/contact) or walk it in.

The Atlantic does not care what resin you paid for. It cares whether the skin is closed. Match the repair to the foam, let it cure, and you get the board back. Match it to a guess from a Facebook group, and you get a heavier mystery.
